% Abstract (recommended length around 80-200 words; this is not a copy of your thesis assignment!)
\def\Abstract{%
Automatic evaluation of machine translation remains challenging, as commonly used metrics correlate imperfectly with human judgment and are sensitive to surface-level variation. This thesis investigates two complementary approaches to improving and analyzing machine translation evaluation: targeted paraphrasing of reference translations and embedding-based evaluation methods. 

The first part demonstrates that controlled paraphrasing of reference translations improves the robustness of string-based metrics, while more complex generation methods do not necessarily outperform simpler ones.

The second part analyzes the geometric properties of sentence embeddings, identifying interpretable structures in which linguistic transformations correspond to consistent directional shifts. We introduce a new dataset of complex sentence transformations for Czech and show that current sentence embedding models rely heavily on surface similarity, and that strong performance on intrinsic evaluation tasks does not reliably predict effectiveness in machine translation evaluation.

Overall, this work highlights the gap between lexical overlap and true semantic equivalence and limitations of current automatic evaluation methods.

}
